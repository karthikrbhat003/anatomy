# 🧘‍♀️ Wheel Pose / Upward Bow (Urdhva Dhanurasana)

*   **Classification:** Sagittal plane spine backbend / full-body extension; Closed Kinetic Chain.
*   **Muscles Involved:**
    *   *Agonists:* **[Erector Spinae](../../muscles/regions/02_back_spine/anatomy.md#3-intermediate-layer-intrinsic-back-muscles)**, **[Rhomboid Major/Minor](../../muscles/regions/02_back_spine/anatomy.md#1-superficial-layer-appendicular-back-muscles)**, **[Gluteus Maximus](../../muscles/regions/06_lower_limb/anatomy.md#1-gluteal-region--deep-hip-rotators)**, **[Quadriceps](../../muscles/regions/06_lower_limb/anatomy.md#2-muscles-of-the-thigh)**.
    *   *Stabilizers:* Transversus Abdominis, Adductor Magnus.
*   **Key Landmarks:** Gluteus maximus inserts into the gluteal tuberosity of the femur and the IT Band; rhomboids insert into the medial border of the scapula.

---

## 🏋️ Proper Execution
1.  Lie face up on your mat, bend your knees, and place your feet flat on the floor close to your hips (parallel, hip-width apart).
2.  Place your hands on the floor next to your ears, fingers pointing toward your shoulders.
3.  Press through your feet and hands to lift your hips and chest, arching your body into a bridge.
4.  Actively push your chest toward the wall behind you, extending through the shoulders.

---

## 🛡️ Joint Safety & Precautions
*   **SI Joint & Lumbar Pinching (Knee Flaring):** Letting your feet turn out and knees flare wide inactivates the hamstrings and over-activates the gluteus maximus, compressing the **sacroiliac (SI) joint** and pinching the **L5-S1 lumbar segment**. Keep feet parallel, thighs squeezing toward midline (engaging the adductors), and lift from the mid-back (Rhomboids/Erectors) to distribute load.
*   **Wrist Loading:** Ensure hands are aligned and weight is distributed across the perimeter of the palm (Hasta Bandha) to avoid carpal compression.

---

## 🧠 Mind-Muscle Connection (Sensory Cues)
*   *Visualize:* Imagine you are squeezing a block between your thighs to keep your legs parallel.
*   *Resist:* Push the floor away through hands and feet to lift the spine.
