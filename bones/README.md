## 🧬 Principles of Bone Anatomy & Physiology

To understand human biomechanics, you must first understand the structural properties of bone tissue:

### 1. Axial vs. Appendicular Skeleton
The human skeleton is divided into two major functional parts:
*   **Axial Skeleton (80 bones):** Form the central axis of the body. They protect the brain, spinal cord, and thoracic organs. Includes the skull, hyoid, vertebral column, ribs, and sternum.
*   **Appendicular Skeleton (126+ bones):** Form the limbs and the girdles (pectoral and pelvic) that anchor the limbs to the axial skeleton. Designed primarily for movement and locomotion.

### 2. Bone Classifications
Bones are classified by their shape, which dictates their mechanical function:
1.  **Long Bones:** Longer than they are wide, acting as levers (e.g., *Humerus*, *Femur*).
2.  **Short Bones:** Cube-shaped, providing stability and support with little movement (e.g., *Carpals*, *Tarsals*).
3.  **Flat Bones:** Thin, curved plates protecting organs and providing broad surfaces for muscle attachment (e.g., *Sternum*, *Scapula*, *Parietal*).
4.  **Irregular Bones:** Complex, non-uniform shapes protecting nervous tissue (e.g., *Vertebrae*, *Sacrum*).
5.  **Sesamoid Bones:** Embedded in tendons to protect them from wear and tear, and to increase mechanical leverage (e.g., *Patella*, sesamoids of the thumb/big toe).

### 3. Understanding Bony Landmarks
Bones are not smooth; they are covered in bumps, ridges, grooves, and holes called **landmarks**. These landmarks are classified into:
*   **Projections / Processes:** Raised markings where muscles, tendons, or ligaments attach (e.g., *Trochanter*, *Tuberosity*, *Condyle*, *Spine*).
*   **Depressions / Openings:** Grooves or holes that house blood vessels and nerves, or form joints (e.g., *Fossa*, *Foramen*, *Sulcus*).

## 🔗 The Skeletomuscular Linkage System: Origins & Insertions
Bones and muscles act together as a lever system to produce movement. In this system:
*   **The Bone acts as the Lever.**
*   **The Joint acts as the Fulcrum (pivot point).**
*   **The Muscle contraction provides the Force.**

### Origins vs. Insertions
Each skeletal muscle attaches to at least two different bones:
1.  **Origin:** The muscle's attachment point on the **relatively stationary bone** during contraction. Usually proximal (closer to the center of the body).
2.  **Insertion:** The muscle's attachment point on the **movable bone** during contraction. Usually distal (further from the center of the body).

When a muscle contracts, it pulls its **insertion** toward its **origin**, producing joint rotation.
